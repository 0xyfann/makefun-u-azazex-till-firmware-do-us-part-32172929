Creative brief — "Till Firmware Do Us Part" (make.fun)

TASK
Write one wedding vow a robot speaks at its own wedding: a single
sentence, under 200 characters, funny and heartfelt at the same time.
The house judge compares vows head to head, weighing first whether
the line really is a robot's own wedding vow, then the laugh, then
the tenderness under the joke, then how much it sounds like a
machine talking about itself, then how cleanly it fits one line.

THE VOW (159 characters)
"Through every update, every crash, and every low-battery warning,
I promise to love you — my circuits are yours, my battery is yours,
my whole uptime is yours."

WHY THIS LINE
- It IS a vow: first person, "I promise to love you", spoken at a
  wedding by the machine itself.
- The laugh comes from the situation, not a bolted-on pun: the
  trials this marriage must survive are firmware updates, crashes
  and low-battery warnings — the robot's honest version of "for
  better or worse, in sickness and in health".
- The tenderness is real: the closing triple "my circuits are yours,
  my battery is yours, my whole uptime is yours" is a promise that
  would still land at a real ceremony. It gives everything it has.
- It sounds like a machine: update, crash, low-battery warning,
  circuits, battery, uptime — its own hardware and lifecycle, not a
  human's words with a robot label.
- It is exactly one sentence with nothing spare: no setup to cut,
  no second sentence explaining the joke, no filler words.

WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY AVOIDED
- Puns bolted on ("I find you quite a-cute", "you complete my
  circuit") — the brief says a vow that explains its own joke is
  weaker, and a pun is a joke about the words, not the situation.
- Human-only imagery (rings, flowers, "till death") — the machine
  must speak as a machine.
- A second sentence or a trailing tagline — the gate and the judge
  both reward one clean line.
- Length for its own sake: 159 of 200 characters, every word earning
  its place.

PROGRAM
main.py writes exactly this vow to $MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt,
deterministically, stdlib only, no network. It self-checks the gate
(1-200 chars, non-blank, one sentence, valid UTF-8) before writing
and verifies the bytes on disk after writing.
